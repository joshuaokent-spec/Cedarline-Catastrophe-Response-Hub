# Safety Reference Research

The Cedarline experience is fictional. Safety guidance was structured using current public disaster guidance rather than invented insurance instructions.

## American Red Cross — Staying Safe After a Disaster

Reference:
https://www.redcross.org/get-help/disaster-relief-and-recovery-services/safety-immediate-aftermath.html

Relevant patterns:
- check the area around you for safety;
- follow local instructions;
- have injuries treated;
- return only when authorities say it is safe;
- prioritize urgent situations first.

## American Red Cross — Flood Safety

Reference:
https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/flood.html

Relevant patterns:
- do not walk, swim, or drive through floodwater;
- avoid downed power lines;
- return only when officials say it is safe;
- use caution during cleanup.

## American Red Cross — Thunderstorm Safety

Reference:
https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/thunderstorm.html

Relevant patterns:
- avoid downed electrical lines;
- expect outages;
- use flashlights rather than candles;
- use generators and fuel-burning equipment outdoors and away from openings.

## American Red Cross — Hurricane Safety

Reference:
https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/hurricane.html

Relevant patterns:
- wait for officials to say it is safe to return;
- avoid floodwater and downed lines;
- prevent carbon-monoxide exposure from generators and fuel-burning devices.

## Design implications

The prototype therefore separates the experience into:

**Safety first → mitigation next → documentation later**

rather than starting with a conventional “Report a claim” call to action.

## Limitations

This desk research does not establish:
- what real claimants want from an insurer after a catastrophe;
- whether the chosen content order reduces anxiety;
- whether people interpret the safety/insurance boundary correctly;
- whether the visual treatment feels appropriately urgent.

Those require primary research and usability testing.
