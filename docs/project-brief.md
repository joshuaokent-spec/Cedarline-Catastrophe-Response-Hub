# Project Brief — Catastrophe Response Hub

## Design challenge

A catastrophe experience is not a normal insurance-service moment.

A customer may be:
- displaced;
- without power;
- looking at visible property damage;
- dealing with active weather;
- unsure whether it is safe to re-enter;
- using a phone with limited battery or connectivity;
- worried about what insurance needs immediately.

The UX problem is:

> **How can an insurer help people separate urgent safety actions from insurance tasks, understand what can wait, and move toward recovery without overwhelming them?**

## Primary design hypothesis

When people are under disaster stress, the experience should prioritize:

1. immediate safety;
2. preventing additional harm when it is safe;
3. documenting only what can be observed safely;
4. starting the claim with incomplete information;
5. adding evidence and details later.

## Business / service goals

- reduce pressure to document damage before conditions are safe;
- help customers understand which tasks can wait;
- connect recovery content to claim reporting;
- create a consistent catastrophe visual language;
- support mobile use and low-attention contexts;
- avoid implying live emergency monitoring or policy-specific coverage decisions.

## Scope

Included:
- safety triage;
- hazard-specific guidance;
- Now / Next / Later recovery sequence;
- safe documentation checklist;
- local checklist persistence;
- printable recovery guide;
- visual system;
- safety-source references.

Out of scope:
- live weather alerts;
- emergency dispatch;
- live catastrophe maps;
- policy-specific coverage determination;
- repair-provider recommendations;
- real claims integration.

- high-stress UX;
