# Crisis Content Strategy

## Principle: safety before service

An insurer should not create pressure to perform insurance tasks while the customer may still be in danger.

The primary sequence is:

**Are you safe? → What happened? → What should happen now? → What can happen next? → What can wait?**

## Content rules

### Lead with consequence, not department language

Preferred:
- “Can you safely remain where you are?”
- “Do not enter visibly unstable structures.”
- “Photograph damage only from a safe location.”

Avoid:
- “Catastrophe claims intake”
- “Loss documentation requirements”
- “Complete FNOL now”

### Make waiting explicit

Useful phrases:
- “When it is safe…”
- “This can wait.”
- “You do not need a complete inventory before starting a claim.”
- “Add additional photos later.”

### Separate safety and coverage

Safety guidance should never depend on whether an event is covered.

Coverage language should remain conditional:
- “may apply”;
- “coverage is determined by policy terms”;
- “report received does not mean coverage approved.”

### Avoid fake urgency

Do not use:
- countdowns;
- red flashing alerts;
- “Act now!” insurance CTAs;
- fake live-weather banners.

The prototype explicitly states that it does not use live alerts.

## Tone

Cedarline catastrophe content should be:
- calm;
- direct;
- short;
- concrete;
- non-judgmental;
- honest about uncertainty.
